THE MAGNA CARTA (1215)

The Magna Carta (Latin for "Great Charter") was sealed on June 15, 1215, at Runnymede, near Windsor, England. King John of England was forced by rebellious barons to agree to this document, which established the principle that everyone, including the king, was subject to the law.

The Magna Carta contained 63 clauses addressing issues such as limits on taxation without the consent of a royal council, protection of church rights, access to swift justice, and restrictions on feudal payments. Clause 39 established the right to a fair trial: no free man could be imprisoned, dispossessed, or exiled except by the lawful judgment of his peers or by the law of the land.

Although many of its clauses were specific to 13th-century feudal issues, the Magna Carta's broader principles of rule of law and limited government profoundly influenced the development of constitutional law worldwide, including the United States Constitution and the Universal Declaration of Human Rights.
