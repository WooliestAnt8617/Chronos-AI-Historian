WORLD WAR I: THE GREAT WAR (1914–1918)

I. THE CAUSES (MAIN AND THE TRIGGER)
The outbreak of World War I was not caused by a single event but by a complex web of long-standing tensions, often summarized by the acronym M.A.I.N.:
1. Militarism: The massive buildup of armed forces and the glorification of military power. Germany and Britain engaged in a naval arms race.
2. Alliances: A system of mutual defense treaties that meant a local conflict could quickly escalate. The Triple Entente (France, Britain, Russia) faced the Triple Alliance (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy).
3. Imperialism: Fierce competition for colonies in Africa and Asia.
4. Nationalism: Intense pride and the desire for self-determination, especially in the Balkans (the "powder keg of Europe").

THE IMMEDIATE TRIGGER: On June 28, 1914, Archduke Franz Ferdinand, heir to the Austro-Hungarian throne, was assassinated in Sarajevo, Bosnia. The assassin was Gavrilo Princip, a member of the Black Hand, a Serbian nationalist group. This set off a "domino effect" of alliance activations.

II. MAJOR PHASES AND BATTLES
1. The War of Movement (1914): Germany executed the Schlieffen Plan, invading neutral Belgium to reach France. They were stopped at the Battle of the Marne.
2. Trench Warfare (1915–1917): The war settled into a stalemate on the Western Front. Soldiers lived in horrific conditions in trenches separated by "No Man's Land."
3. Key Battles:
   - Battle of Verdun (1916): One of the longest and costliest battles (700,000+ casualties).
   - Battle of the Somme (1916): British and French offensive that resulted in over 1 million total casualties.
   - Battle of Gallipoli (1915): A failed Allied attempt to knock the Ottoman Empire out of the war.

III. TECHNOLOGY AND INNOVATION
WWI saw the first mass use of:
- Chemical Warfare (Poison gas).
- Tanks (First used by the British in 1916).
- Submarines (German U-boats).
- Airplanes (Transitioned from scouting to aerial "dogfights").

IV. THE END OF THE WAR
- The United States entered in April 1917, providing fresh troops and resources.
- Russia withdrew in late 1917 following the Bolshevik Revolution.
- The Armistice: On November 11, 1918, fighting ended.
- Treaty of Versailles (1919): Formally ended the war but imposed heavy reparations and the "War Guilt Clause" on Germany, which many historians believe contributed to the rise of WWII.
